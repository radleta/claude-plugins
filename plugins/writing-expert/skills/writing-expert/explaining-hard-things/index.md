# Explaining Hard Things

Knowledge hub covering the craft of making complex technical ideas legible to informed-but-not-expert readers: diagnosing the curse of knowledge (when you've forgotten what it's like not to know), building analogies that illuminate rather than mislead, and integrating code samples or technical details into running prose without losing narrative momentum.

Sources: Steven Pinker, *The Sense of Style* (2014); George Gopen & Judith Swan, "The Science of Scientific Writing" (American Scientist, 1990).

## Pages

- [curse-of-knowledge](curse-of-knowledge.md) — The expert's core blind spot: once you know something, you cannot reliably simulate not knowing it. Diagnostic signals and concrete countermeasures.
- [classic-style-peer-model](classic-style-peer-model.md) — Writer as peer pointing at something real, not teacher lecturing. The mental posture shift that is the most effective antidote to the curse of knowledge.
- [topic-stress-architecture](topic-stress-architecture.md) — Every sentence has a topic position (familiar context, beginning) and a stress position (new information, end). Matching structure to reader expectation is the sentence-level mechanism of clarity.
- [analogy-construction](analogy-construction.md) — Analogies work via structural mapping, not surface resemblance. The three-step test and the "unlike" clause for marking where the analogy breaks.
- [code-in-prose](code-in-prose.md) — The three-part frame (before/code/after), minimum viable code samples, interleaving vs. monolith, and when to omit code entirely.
