# Clutter Removal: The Bracket-and-Cut Audit

Zinsser's central argument in *On Writing Well* is that clutter is the disease of prose, and the cure is a ruthless word-by-word audit. The method is not a vague instruction to "tighten the writing" — it is a dedicated pass with a specific test for every word: if a word can be removed without losing meaning or damaging rhythm, it goes.

## The Bracket Technique

Zinsser describes bracketing everything the piece could lose — a physical or mental gesture that separates the question "does this belong?" from the act of deleting. The bracket is provisional; the deletion is final. Reviewing bracketed passages before cutting prevents over-pruning and catches cases where the bracket was wrong.

## Clutter Taxonomy

Clutter concentrates in predictable categories:

| Category | Pattern | Cut to |
|----------|---------|--------|
| Weak qualifiers | "rather," "very," "somewhat," "a bit," "quite" | Drop or rewrite with a stronger noun or verb |
| Redundant pairs | "each and every," "first and foremost," "various and sundry" | Pick one word from the pair |
| Throat-clearing preamble | "In today's world," "It goes without saying that," "The fact of the matter is" | Delete; start at the substance |
| Over-hedged attribution | "It would seem that," "It could be argued that" | Commit directly or attribute by name |
| Backward reference | "as I mentioned earlier," "as we have seen" | Trust the reader; delete |
| Preposition pileups | "in terms of," "with regard to," "as it relates to" | Replace with "about," "for," "on" |

Source: Zinsser, *On Writing Well*, chapters on Simplicity and Clutter.

## The Guiding Test

For each candidate cut: does the sentence still say what it meant? Does it still sound like the writer meant it? If yes to both, the word was clutter.

Zinsser's secondary test: read the sentence aloud after cutting. If a rhythm problem surfaces, restore the word — rhythm is a legitimate reason to keep a word even when it carries no semantic weight. The goal is meaningful density, not minimum word count.

## How to apply

1. Complete all structural passes before cutting clutter. Pruning prose that will be restructured wastes effort.
2. Run the clutter audit as its own dedicated editing pass — do not combine it with structural revision or diction work.
3. Identify your personal clutter patterns first (hedges, intensifiers, preamble phrases) and search for them explicitly rather than rereading the whole piece linearly.
4. For each candidate word: (a) remove it mentally, (b) test for meaning loss, (c) test for rhythm damage. Keep only if one test fails.
5. Reserve "quite," "rather," "somewhat," and "very" for the rare moment where the qualifier is itself the point. Default: delete.
6. After cutting, re-read the full sentence — occasionally a cut exposes a structural gap that the clutter was masking, and that gap needs repair, not more cutting.
