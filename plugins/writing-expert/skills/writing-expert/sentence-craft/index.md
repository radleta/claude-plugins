# Sentence Craft

Knowledge hub covering the line-level moves that determine whether prose reads well: how to vary sentence rhythm to avoid monotony, how to cut without losing meaning, how to choose the precise word over the approximate one, and how to identify and cut the darlings (the passages you love that the reader doesn't need).

## Pages

- [clutter-removal](clutter-removal.md) — Zinsser's bracket-and-cut audit: systematic removal of every word not doing work, with a clutter taxonomy and the rhythm-safety test.
- [sentence-rhythm](sentence-rhythm.md) — Length variation, stress position, and the detonation pattern (short sentence after long); McPhee, Zinsser, and Pinker's convergent technique.
- [diction-precision](diction-precision.md) — McPhee's dictionary-pass discipline: approximate words signal approximate thinking; the method for finding the exact word, not the impressive one.
- [nominalization-traps](nominalization-traps.md) — Pinker's support-verb + abstract-noun diagnostic and verb-restoration technique; active-voice recovery and the three legitimate uses of passive.
