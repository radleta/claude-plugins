# Reporting Surplus: Gather More Than You Will Use

John McPhee's pre-writing discipline in *Draft No. 4*: before writing a word of prose, exhaust the subject. Interview more people than you will quote, read more background than you will cite, generate more examples than the piece can hold. McPhee's working ratio is roughly 4:1 — four units of gathered material for every one that appears in print.

The reason is selection power. When you write from a surplus, every cut strengthens the piece because you are selecting the best from a large pool. When you write from scarcity, every sentence fights to stay in regardless of quality, and you fill structural gaps with padding rather than the right material.

The surplus also changes what you discover. The fifth interview often contradicts the first four. The document you find after "enough" research is the document that reframes everything. Stopping early preserves your first impression; exhaustive reporting gives you a corrected one.

For technical writing (not journalism), the same principle holds: gather every edge case, failure mode, alternative approach, and counterargument before writing. The ones that do not appear in the piece are not wasted — they shape which claims you can confidently make and which you must hedge.

**Source:** John McPhee, *Draft No. 4* (reporting chapters; the gathering-before-writing discipline).

## How to apply

1. **Before writing, list what you know vs. what you need to know.** Research until the "need to know" column is empty or the gaps are deliberate choices.
2. **Gather in a separate document, not the draft file.** Keep raw notes, quotes, and examples in a staging area; the draft file starts empty.
3. **Apply the 4:1 target:** for a 1,000-word piece, aim to have gathered material that could support 4,000 words. You will discard 75%; the 25% you keep will be the right 25%.
4. **Do the uncomfortable research last** — the source you are most tempted to skip is often the one that reframes the piece.
5. **Mark your notes before drafting:** label each item "must use," "could use," or "cuts if needed." This converts selection from intuition into a deliberate pass.
