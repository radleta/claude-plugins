# The Multi-Pass Writing Sequence

The most common process failure is collapsing multiple passes into one: writing and revising simultaneously, or line-editing before the structure is sound. This produces prose that is locally polished but globally broken — beautiful sentences in the wrong order making an argument that does not land.

Synthesized from McPhee (*Draft No. 4*), Zinsser (*On Writing Well*), and Lamott (*Bird by Bird*), the durable sequence is:

| Pass | Job | Failure mode if skipped or merged |
|------|-----|----------------------------------|
| 1. Reporting | Gather 3–4× the material you'll use | Writing from scarcity; padding |
| 2. Spine | Decide structure; write it as a document | Drafting into structural uncertainty |
| 3. Rough draft | Get it all out; inner critic off | Draft becomes revision — slow, blocked |
| 4. Structural revision | Move, cut, reorder whole sections | Line-editing prose that will be deleted |
| 5. Line edit | Sentence rhythm, word choice, concision | — |
| 6. Opening + title | Write these last, after the piece knows itself | Opening promises what the piece cannot deliver |

**Why the sequence matters:**

Structural revision must precede line editing: you cannot fix sentences that belong to sections that will be deleted. Structure first; sentences second.

Opening last: the opening makes a promise. You cannot know what promise the piece can keep until the piece exists. Zinsser observes that most writers spend disproportionate time on an opening that does not survive the piece's own development. McPhee writes his opening only after the structure and draft are complete.

Rough draft before revision: revision requires seeing the whole; drafting produces it. They require incompatible cognitive states — production vs. evaluation.

**Sources:** William Zinsser, *On Writing Well* (draft discipline, opening chapters); John McPhee, *Draft No. 4* (structure, reporting); Anne Lamott, *Bird by Bird* (first drafts, process permission).

## How to apply

1. **Name the pass you are in before you start.** Do not switch passes mid-session. If you notice yourself line-editing while structurally revising, stop and decide which pass is primary for this session.
2. **Use separate documents for reporting notes and the draft.** The reporting document is never edited down — it feeds the draft, which is a separate file.
3. **Do structural revision in a duplicate file or on a printed copy** so you cannot accidentally line-edit while cutting sections.
4. **Write the opening and title in a final dedicated session** after the full draft is complete and structurally sound. The opening should match the piece that exists, not the piece you planned.
5. **When blocked in any pass, ask: is this a pass-collision?** If you are blocked in drafting because you are judging quality, you have merged drafting and revision — separate them into two sessions.
